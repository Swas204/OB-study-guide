# Organisational Behaviour · COM401-8 · Study Guide

A complete walkthrough of the **COM401-8 Organisational Behaviour** syllabus (CHRIST University,
BCom Honours, Semester VII). Every heading in the syllabus document, explained in plain language
with a worked example for each concept and a diagram wherever the idea has moving parts.

## How to use it

- **Read the diagram first.** Each framework is drawn before it is explained. You will remember the
  shape in the exam hall and rebuild the content from it.
- **Coloured definition boxes** are worth learning close to word for word. Everything else you can
  put in your own words.
- **Yellow boxes are exam tips**, usually the criticism or comparison that separates a full-mark
  answer from a half-mark one.
- **Red boxes are traps**, including several findings that are the opposite of what most people
  assume.

## Reading tools

The bar at the top of every unit page has:

- **Hide contents** to close the sidebar while you read.
- **Mark as done** at the end of every section, with a progress count at the top.
- **Undone only** to hide finished sections and go back through what you skipped.
- **Light / dark theme.** Dark is the default.

Progress is saved in your own browser, so it stays on that device and resets if you clear site data.

## Sources

Built from the syllabus document plus the prescribed reading, with **Robbins &amp; Judge,
*Organizational Behavior*, 19th ed.** as the spine, Hofstede for the cultural dimensions, and
Goleman for emotional intelligence. McShane &amp; Von Glinow, Luthans, and Nelson &amp; Quick were
used for the MARS model, psychological capital, and the stress chapter respectively.

## Two honest caveats

1. **This is not official course material** and it is not a substitute for your professor's notes
   or lectures. Where his version differs, his version is the one the paper is set from.
2. **Six headings in this syllabus are newer than all eight prescribed textbooks** and are not
   covered by any of them: green organisational behaviour, environmental psychology at work,
   technostress and AI-driven stress, AI in managerial decision-making, green teams, and
   behavioural change for sustainability. Those sections were written from the wider research
   literature, so treat them as well-researched rather than textbook-verifiable.
