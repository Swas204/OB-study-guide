How the course fits together

The five units are not five separate lists. They are one argument that climbs three levels of analysis, and knowing that makes the whole paper easier to hold in your head.

Read this before you start

Three themes were added to this syllabus recently and appear in every single unit: sustainability and green behaviour, ethics and human values, and artificial intelligence at work. Older question papers and older editions of the textbooks will not cover them well. These are exactly the topics an examiner uses to separate a first class from a distinction, so do not treat them as optional extras at the end of each unit.

Course outcomes, and what they actually demand

The verb in each outcome tells you the depth of answer expected. Two of the five outcomes sit at Evaluate (L5), which means describing the concept earns only part of the marks. You have to judge it.

How to answer an L5 "evaluate" question

Use four moves in order: define the concept in one or two lines, explain its mechanism with an example, criticise it with at least two specific limitations, then judge it with a conditional verdict. The conditional verdict is the part most students skip, and it reads like this: "It works where the task is X and the workforce is Y, and it fails where Z." That sentence alone lifts a description into an evaluation.

Exam pattern and what it implies

Internal assessment · 90 marks
CIA I, II and III
  • CIA I:20 marks. Quizzes, role plays, objective tests, written assignments, discussion forums, article reviews, case analysis.
  • CIA II:50 marks. Mid-semester written exam, 2 hours. Section A: 3 × 5 = 15 (third question has internal choice). Section B: 2 × 10 = 20 (one internal choice). Section C: 1 case study × 15, compulsory.
  • CIA III:20 marks. Group work: presentation, viva voce and report submission.
End semester · 100 marks · 3 hours
ESE structure
  • Section A:7 × 5 = 35. Short answers, all seven compulsory, with internal choice only in questions 6 and 7.
  • Section B:5 × 10 = 50. Conceptual, descriptive or problem type, all five compulsory, two of them with internal choice.
  • Section C:1 × 15 = 15. Case study, compulsory.
What the pattern tells you to do
  • Section A is where selective study dies. Seven short answers with choice in only two of them means five questions are forced. Across five units, that is essentially one compulsory question per unit. You cannot skip a unit.
  • Section C is 15 marks on one compulsory case. That is more than any single essay. Practise applying concepts to a messy situation, not just reciting them. Every unit page here ends with a case prompt and an answer skeleton for this reason.
  • Definitions are worth memorising verbatim. A 5-mark answer that opens with the exact textbook definition, then gives a diagram or example, then two points of significance, is a complete answer. Do not write a page.
  • Diagrams earn marks in Section B. Where a framework is drawn in this guide, reproduce the drawing, label it, then explain it.

A gap you should know about

None of the eight prescribed books covers the newest headings well, because most predate them: green organisational behaviour, environmental psychology at work, technostress, AI in managerial decision-making, green teams and behavioural change for sustainability. Those sections in this guide are built from the wider research literature rather than from a single chapter you can look up. If your professor's notes cover them, his version is the one to follow for the exam, since he sets the paper.

How to use this guide

01
Read the diagram first

Each framework is drawn before it is explained. Look at the shape, then read. You will remember the shape in the exam hall and rebuild the content from it.

02
Definitions are boxed

Anything in a coloured definition box is worth learning close to word for word. Everything else you can put in your own words.

03
Examples are labelled

Every concept has one worked example, mostly Indian workplace situations. In an answer, one concrete example is worth more than a third paragraph of theory.

04
Yellow boxes are exam tips

These flag the specific point that separates a full-mark answer from a half-mark one, usually a criticism or a comparison students leave out.

05
Red boxes are traps

Common confusions and things students get backwards, for instance what the research on age and performance actually found.

06
End of each unit

A 5-mark bank, a 10-mark bank, and a case prompt with an answer outline.

Suggested order

Work through the units in order the first time, because Unit 5 assumes the individual-level vocabulary from Units 2 and 3. For revision, reverse it: start with Unit 5, since power, conflict, culture and change carry the most marks in Section B and are the easiest to write at length on.
